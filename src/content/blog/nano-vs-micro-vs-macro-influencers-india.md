# Nano vs Micro vs Macro Influencers in India

## Quick Answer

**Nano influencers have roughly 1,000–10,000 followers, micro 10,000–100,000, macro 100,000–1,000,000, and mega above a million — and engagement rate falls as you move up while cost rises.** For most Indian brands the decision is not "which tier is best" but "which tier matches the job": nano and micro creators win on trust, cost, and local density, while macro and mega creators win on reach and speed. The tiers are conventions, not standards — different platforms and agencies draw the lines slightly differently, so always confirm what a rate card means by follower band rather than by label.

## The Tiers at a Glance

| Tier | Followers | Typical engagement | Relative cost | Best at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | 1k–10k | Highest (often 4–8%) | Lowest, sometimes barter | Local trust, dense city coverage, product seeding |
| **Micro** | 10k–100k | High (2–6%) | Low to moderate | Niche authority with real reach — the default for most campaigns |
| **Macro** | 100k–1M | Moderate (1–3%) | High | Category-wide reach, launch moments |
| **Mega** | 1M+ | Lowest (often under 1.5%) | Highest | Mass awareness, brand-building at scale |

Two things drive the pattern. Smaller audiences are more genuinely connected to the creator, so a higher share engages. And smaller creators take fewer brand deals, so each one carries more credibility rather than reading as another ad.

Note that these ranges are conventions. Some rate cards call anything under 50k "micro". Confirm the follower band, not the word.

## Why Engagement Falls as Followers Rise

This trips up brands who assume reach scales linearly with price. It does not, for three reasons:

- **Audience dilution.** A 5k account's followers mostly chose to follow for a specific reason. A 900k account has accumulated followers across years and topic drift, many of whom are no longer interested.
- **Parasocial distance.** Nano creators reply to comments individually. That reciprocity produces engagement that a creator with 40,000 comments cannot reproduce.
- **Ad fatigue.** Larger creators post more paid partnerships, and audiences discount them accordingly.

The practical consequence: **cost per engaged viewer often falls as you go down the tiers, even though cost per follower rises.** A macro creator is usually cheaper per follower and more expensive per person who actually cares.

## Which Tier for Which Job

### Choose nano when

You are a local business, your catchment is one city or a few neighbourhoods, you have physical product to seed, or you want volume of authentic mentions rather than a single big moment. Ten nano creators in one city frequently outperform one macro creator nationally for a restaurant, salon, gym, or local retail brand — the audience overlap is real and the total cost is lower.

The tradeoff is coordination. Ten deals is ten briefs, ten contracts, and ten payments.

### Choose micro when

You want a defensible balance of reach and credibility, or you are running a category-specific campaign — skincare, fitness, regional food, budget tech. This is the default tier for most Indian brands and the reason it is the most competitive to book. Micro creators are professional enough to deliver on brief and small enough that their recommendation still reads as personal.

### Choose macro when

You need a large audience reached quickly, you are launching something that benefits from a visible moment, or you need one recognisable name to anchor a campaign that micro creators then amplify. Macro creators also tend to have the most reliable production quality, which matters when the content will be reused as paid ads.

### Choose mega when

Your objective is mass awareness and your budget is built for it. At this tier you are buying reach and association, not engagement rate, and the economics only work for large brands with genuinely national products.

## The Mixed-Tier Approach

Most well-run campaigns do not pick one tier. A common structure:

1. **One macro creator** anchors the campaign and produces the highest-quality asset.
2. **Five to ten micro creators** carry the message into specific niches.
3. **Twenty-plus nano creators** create density, so the product appears repeatedly in a defined audience's feed rather than once.

Repetition across tiers is what makes a campaign feel like a moment instead of an ad. Seeing something once from a large account is forgettable; seeing it three times from three people you follow is not.

## What Each Tier Costs in India

Rates vary enormously by niche, city, deliverable, and usage rights, and any single number is misleading without that context. For current ranges by follower band and deliverable type, see the [micro influencer pricing guide for India](/blog/micro-influencer-pricing-guide-india-2026).

Two rules hold across every tier:

- **Usage rights change the price more than follower count does.** Permission to run the content as a paid ad for six months can double or triple a rate. Agree this before quoting.
- **Exclusivity is a separate line item.** "Do not work with competitors for 90 days" is a real cost to a creator and should be priced, not assumed.

## Where Brands Get This Wrong

- **Treating follower count as the price.** Two creators at 40k can legitimately differ 4x in rate based on niche, engagement, and production quality.
- **Assuming bigger is safer.** Macro creators have higher minimums and lower engagement; if the campaign underperforms, more budget is exposed.
- **Ignoring audience city.** A macro creator with a national audience is a poor fit for a single-location business at any price. Audience location outranks tier for local brands.
- **Scaling nano campaigns without process.** Twenty nano creators means twenty contracts and twenty payments. Without a way to standardise terms and payment, the admin cost eats the tier's cost advantage — see [how to pay influencers safely in India](/blog/how-to-pay-influencers-safely-india-2026).

## Common Questions

**Are nano influencers worth it if their reach is so small?**
Yes, when you buy several. The unit is not one nano creator, it is a cohort of them covering a defined audience. One nano creator is a rounding error; fifteen in one city is a campaign.

**What is the difference between micro and mid-tier?**
"Mid-tier" is an informal label some agencies use for roughly 50k–500k, overlapping the top of micro and the bottom of macro. It is not a standard tier — ask for the follower band.

**Does the tier matter more than the niche?**
No. Niche fit and audience location both outrank tier. A perfectly-sized creator with the wrong audience delivers nothing.

**Do these ranges apply outside Instagram?**
The follower bands are broadly used across YouTube and Instagram, but engagement benchmarks differ by platform — YouTube engagement rates are calculated differently and are not comparable to Instagram percentages.
