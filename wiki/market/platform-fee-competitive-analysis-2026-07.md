# Platform Fee Competitive Analysis — July 2026

**Prepared by:** Tejas Mehta, CMO  
**Date:** July 24, 2026  
**Purpose:** Evaluate Influora's 15% creator fee relative to market benchmarks

---

## Executive Summary

Influora currently charges creators a **15% platform fee** on deal value (no brand-side fee). Based on comprehensive market research across 15+ influencer marketing platforms and general marketplace benchmarks, this analysis finds:

**Key Findings:**
- **Creator-side fees range from 0% to 30%**, with most influencer marketplaces charging **10-20%**
- **Influora's 15% sits in the middle of the market range** — neither aggressively low nor prohibitively high
- **Most enterprise platforms (Upfluence, GRIN, Klear) use SaaS subscription models** ($2,000-$5,000/month) rather than transaction fees, targeting brands with large budgets
- **Direct competitor marketplaces (Creator.co, Collabstr, Insense) charge 5-20% to creators**, with many implementing dual-sided fees
- **General marketplace benchmarks confirm 10-20% as standard** for service-based platforms

**Recommendation:**  
Influora's 15% creator fee is **defensible and competitively positioned**. No immediate adjustment required, but consider introducing a **dual-sided fee model** (small brand fee + reduced creator fee) or **tiered pricing** for high-volume creators to match emerging market trends.

## 2. Market Positioning Analysis

### Where Does Influora's 15% Creator Fee Sit?

```
LOW ←―――――――――――――――― MARKET RANGE ――――――――――――――――→ HIGH
0%           5%       10%      15%      20%      25%      30%
│            │        │        │        │        │        │
Enterprise   Collabstr Creator.co INFLUORA Fiverr  Insense Billo (UGC)
SaaS (0%)    Premium  (5% dual) (15%)   (20%)   Trial    (40-60%)
             (5%)                                (20%)
```

**Position:** **Mid-market / Competitive**

- **Below:** Fiverr (20%), Insense Trial (20%), Billo UGC (40-60%)
- **At/Near:** Upwork (10%), Creator.co dual-sided (5%+5%=10% total), Collabstr Basic (10% brand-side)
- **Above:** Enterprise SaaS (0% transaction fee, but $30k-$100k/year contracts)

---

### Competitive Strengths of 15%

1. **Simpler than dual-sided models:** One-sided fee = easier mental math for creators ("I keep 85% of every deal")
2. **No brand-side friction:** Unlike Creator.co (5%+5%), brands pay creators directly without platform markup visibility
3. **Lower than freelance benchmarks:** Fiverr (20%) and Upwork (effective 12-20% with hidden costs) are higher
4. **Sustainable unit economics:** 15% allows platform to fund support, payments, and growth without squeezing creators

---

### Competitive Weaknesses of 15%

1. **Higher than emerging dual-sided models:** Creator.co's 5%+5% feels "fairer" to creators (though total cost to brands is the same)
2. **No volume discounts:** High-performing creators on Influora pay the same 15% whether they close 1 deal or 50
3. **No brand-side revenue:** Missing potential revenue stream from brand subscriptions or small transaction fees
4. **Perception vs. reality:** Creators may compare to "5%" without realizing dual-sided models extract 10% total

## 3. Market Standards & Benchmarks (2026)

### General Marketplace Commission Benchmarks

According to industry-wide marketplace research:

- **Physical goods marketplaces:** 5-20% (average: 12%)
- **Service-based platforms:** 10-30% (average: 18%)
- **Gig economy (Uber, TaskRabbit):** 15-30%
- **Digital products:** 15-30%
- **Rental platforms:** 3-15%

**Source:** [Shipturtle (2026 Marketplace Commission Guide)](https://www.shipturtle.com/blog/marketplace-commission-charges-industry-vendors-shipturtle-2026), [Origami (Marketplace Take Rate Guide)](https://origami-marketplace.com/en-gb/marketplace-take-rate-a-guide-for-marketplace-operators/)

**Influora's Position:** At **15%**, Influora sits **at the low end of service-based platform benchmarks (10-30%)** and **mid-range for influencer-specific marketplaces (5-20%)**.

---

### Influencer Marketplace Fee Trends (2026)

Research across 20+ influencer platforms reveals:

- **10-20% is the dominant range** for pay-per-use marketplaces ([Behind the Scenes](https://behindthescenes.com/blogs/what-are-platform-fees-definition-examples-how-it-works-2026))
- **Dual-sided models (5-10% each side) are gaining traction** to appear "fairer" while maintaining 10-20% total revenue
- **Hidden fees are common:** Payment processing (2-3%), currency conversion (1-3%), and withdrawal fees add 5-10% to stated rates ([Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@platform.jobbers.io/i-analyzed-every-major-freelance-platform-for-2026-heres-what-nobody-tells-you-about-their-fees-a3b1afdc4d81))
- **Volume-based tiers are emerging:** Platforms reward high-volume creators with reduced fees (e.g., Insense 20% → 7% across plan tiers)

## 4. Recommendations

### Option 1: Hold at 15% (RECOMMENDED)

**Rationale:**
- 15% is **competitively positioned** in the middle of the 10-20% market range
- **No immediate pressure** to adjust — not an outlier in either direction
- **Simple, transparent model** is easier to communicate than tiered/dual-sided structures
- **Focus on product/market fit first** — pricing optimization comes after achieving scale

**Action:** Monitor competitor pricing quarterly; revisit if market shifts below 12% average.

---

### Option 2: Introduce Dual-Sided Fee (5% Brand + 10% Creator = 15% Total)

**Rationale:**
- **Matches Creator.co's model** (though they charge 5%+5%=10% total)
- **Appears "fairer"** to creators (10% < 15%) even though total revenue is the same
- **Unlocks brand-side pricing optimization** (could test 3-7% brand fee independently)

**Risks:**
- **Increases brand-side friction** (visible fee at checkout may deter conversions)
- **More complex messaging** ("Why am I paying a platform fee as a brand?")
- **May not materially change creator adoption** if total take-home is similar

**Action:** A/B test dual-sided model (5%+10%) vs. current (0%+15%) with new users for 60 days.

---

### Option 3: Tiered Creator Fees (Volume-Based Discounts)

**Rationale:**
- **Retain top creators** by rewarding loyalty/volume (e.g., 15% for 0-5 deals/month, 12% for 6-15 deals, 10% for 16+ deals)
- **Mirrors Insense model** (20% Trial → 10% Premium → 7% Top Tier)
- **Incentivizes creator activity** (lower fee = more reason to close deals on Influora vs. direct outreach)

**Risks:**
- **Complexity in billing/accounting** (per-creator fee tracking)
- **May cannibalize revenue** if high-volume creators were already closing deals at 15%

**Action:** Implement tiered structure once platform reaches 100+ active creators closing 500+ deals/month.

---

### Option 4: Hybrid Model (Subscription + Reduced Fee)

**Rationale:**
- **Diversify revenue streams:** Charge creators $29-$99/month for premium features (priority listing, analytics, verified badge) + reduced 10% fee
- **Predictable MRR** from subscriptions + transaction revenue from deals
- **Targets serious creators** willing to invest in their business

**Risks:**
- **Adds friction to creator onboarding** (free-to-list is a key conversion driver)
- **Requires premium features that justify subscription cost** (not yet built)

**Action:** Phase 2 consideration after achieving product-market fit on free-to-list model.

---

## 5. Conclusion

**Influora's 15% creator fee is market-appropriate and defensible.** It sits comfortably within the 10-20% industry standard for influencer marketplaces and service-based platforms. No immediate change is required.

**Strategic Considerations:**

1. **Monitor dual-sided fee adoption** — if Creator.co's model (5%+5%) becomes dominant, consider testing it
2. **Plan for tiered pricing** — once Influora scales to 100+ creators, volume-based discounts will help retain top performers
3. **Avoid brand-side fees initially** — they add friction; focus on creator-side monetization until GMV scales
4. **Benchmark quarterly** — platform fees are competitive levers; track shifts in Collabstr, Creator.co, and new entrants
